Equivalence of Locally Connected Definitions

I usually like to play with definitions. Given two definitions, I enjoy figuring out which one is stronger, which one is weaker, or whether both are actually equivalent. I recently found one such problem regarding locally connected spaces.

Let's define two definitions for a locally connected space (LCP):

Def 1: A topological space $X$ is said to be locally connected if for each $x \in X$ and for each open neighbourhood $U$ containing $x$, there exists a connected neighbourhood $V$ such that $x \in V \subseteq U$.
Def 2: A topological space $X$ is said to be locally connected if for each $x \in X$ and for each open neighbourhood $U$ containing $x$, there exists a connected open neighbourhood $V$ such that $x \in V \subseteq U$.

Question: Are Def 1 and Def 2 equivalent?

Def 2 definitely implies Def 1, since every open neighbourhood is a neighbourhood. The interesting part is proving the reverse.

Proof that Def 1 implies Def 2

Assume Def 1 holds for the space $X$.

  1. Step 1 & 2: Notice that any open subset $U \subseteq X$ inherits the property from Def 1. We want to show that the connected components of any open set $U$ are open in $X$.
  2. Step 3: Let $C$ be a connected component of an open set $U$. For any $y \in C$, since $U$ is an open neighbourhood of $y$, Def 1 guarantees a connected neighbourhood $N$ such that $y \in N \subseteq U$. Since $C$ is the maximal connected subset of $U$ containing $y$, it must be that $N \subseteq C$. Because $N$ is a neighbourhood of $y$, it contains an open set around $y$. Thus, $C$ is open.
  3. Step 4: Now, for each $x \in X$ and for each open neighbourhood $U$ of $x$, take $V$ to be the connected component of $x$ in $U$. $V$ is connected, and by the previous steps, $V$ is open since it is open in $U$. Since $x \in V \subseteq U$, Def 2 is satisfied.
