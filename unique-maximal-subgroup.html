Unique Maximal Subgroup

Problem Let $G$ be a finite group. If $G$ has a unique maximal subgroup $M$, then the order of $G$ is a prime power (i.e., $G$ is a $p$-group).
Proof.

Let $M$ be the unique maximal subgroup of $G$. Since every proper subgroup of a finite group is contained in some maximal subgroup, and $M$ is the only maximal subgroup, it follows that $M$ contains every proper subgroup of $G$.

Suppose $G$ is not cyclic. Then for every element $x \in G$, the cyclic subgroup generated by $x$, denoted $\langle x \rangle$, must be a proper subgroup of $G$. Therefore, by our previous observation, $\langle x \rangle \le M$ for all $x \in G$. But this implies that every element of $G$ is contained in $M$, meaning $G = M$. This is a contradiction, since a maximal subgroup is by definition a proper subgroup. Thus, $G$ must be cyclic.
